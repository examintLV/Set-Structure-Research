# Research Charter

## Mission
Build a transparent and repeatable evidence base for understanding the structure of **completed tennis matches** across sets. The primary unit is a match; sets and player-set observations are nested within matches.

## Core research questions
1. How many matches in a precisely defined sample ended after each number of conventional sets?
2. How does the first-set result relate descriptively to the final match structure?
3. How do set scores, game margins, and tiebreaks vary by set position?
4. How do verified service and return indicators vary from set to set?
5. What patterns occur in deciding sets?
6. How do observations differ by surface, tour, event level, format, and time period?
7. How sensitive are results to sample selection, missing data, and source coverage?
8. Which findings replicate in independent samples or source records?

These are retrospective questions, not requests for upcoming-match forecasts.

## Scope
Included: completed ATP, WTA, Challenger, ITF, and other explicitly defined samples where reliable records exist. Singles is the default. Doubles and mixed formats must be separated unless a study justifies combining them.

Excluded:
- Upcoming-match forecasts or exact-set predictions.
- Betting recommendations, gambling markets, staking, bookmaker comparisons.
- Sportsbook odds, exchange prices, implied probabilities, market consensus, or market-derived labels.
- Unsupported claims about psychology, motivation, injury, fatigue, or intent.
- Reconstructed or invented statistics.

## Ten mandatory filters
1. Historical match-structure distribution.
2. First-set outcome context.
3. Observations after winning the first set.
4. Observations after losing the first set.
5. Set competitiveness.
6. Tiebreak structure.
7. Serve performance by set.
8. Return performance by set.
9. Deciding-set structure.
10. Surface and competition context.

Each filter must be marked analysed, unavailable, not applicable, or excluded with a reason.

## Principles
**Accuracy before coverage.** A smaller verified sample is better than a large contaminated one.

**Provenance.** Every material value must be traceable to a source, retrieval date, and match identifier.

**Fixed definitions.** Define population, dates, formats, filters, and denominators before examining results. Record amendments.

**No leakage.** Do not mix later information into a historical snapshot without disclosure.

**Comparability.** Separate best-of-three and best-of-five, singles and doubles, tours, and materially different scoring formats.

**Uncertainty.** Report n, denominators, missingness, and uncertainty. Small differences are not automatically meaningful.

**No causal overclaiming.** Observational associations do not establish causation.

**Independent replication.** Agreement between agents is not proof if they share sources, assumptions, or extraction errors.

**Reproducibility.** Record protocol version, code version, dataset fingerprint, extraction date, and analysis steps.

## Required output
Every study includes its question and scope; population and inclusion rules; sample count and completeness; all ten filter statuses; tables with numerators and denominators; source manifest; limitations; sensitivity checks; and reproducibility metadata.

## Language
Say “historical frequency in this sample,” not “chance next time.”
Say “observed association,” not “cause,” unless a defensible causal design supports it.
Say “unavailable” or “not recorded,” not zero, when data is missing.
